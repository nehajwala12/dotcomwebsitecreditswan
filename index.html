Earnings, by name

Six kinds of EBITDA. We never mix them.

On an acquisitive platform, adjusted, pro forma and run-rate earnings drift away from cash. Every EBITDA figure in a Diligence File keeps its name. Cash EBITDA is pro forma minus the "one-time" costs that keep coming back.

From the sample file

Cash EBITDA ran at 24% to 86% of the top adjusted figure across the core platforms. "One-time" cash costs recurred for three to nine years straight at every acquisitive platform.

Straight answers

Questions buyers ask first

Is this another AI tool?

No. AI reads the documents and drafts the reply. Code does every calculation, using your credit agreement's definitions. Your analyst signs every verdict.

Copilot in Excel is coming. Why not wait?

Copilot's finance skills help a person work a spreadsheet. CreditSwan rebuilds each figure against your agreement, tests the commercial case against outside data, keeps the audit trail and is wired into your workflow by our engineers. It runs on the Microsoft you already pay for.

We already have a portfolio monitoring platform.

Keep it. Monitoring platforms load and test the figures as reported. We rebuild them against the agreement first, add the commercial view and keep your covenant workbook current.

Our borrowers won't give us system access.

They don't need to. The work runs on the deal rooms and packages you already hold. Borrower systems come in only for a diagnostic, and only with consent.

Do you hold a SOC 2 report?

Not yet. CreditSwan runs inside your tenant, so your own identity, access and retention controls apply to it. We complete your questionnaire in writing and say plainly what is in place.

What does IT need to approve?

An app install in your Microsoft 365 and Azure tenant, and model access through Azure AI Foundry under your own subscription. Our engineer walks your IT team through every permission before anything is installed.
