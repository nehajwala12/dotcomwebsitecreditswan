CREDITSWAN · COMMERCIAL DILIGENCE

05

Commercial diligence is the signature. Most firms buy it for their biggest deals, once, before close. CreditSwan builds it into every Diligence File. The thesis is split into claims a committee can rule on, and each claim is tested against the deal room, outside evidence and the firm's own past deals. There are forty commercial angles, from foot traffic to franchise health; each deal uses the ones its economics turn on. Each claim gets one of four rulings: supported, partly supported, contradicted, or not enough evidence yet. The evidence comes from kinds of licensed and public sources, read inside the firm's own Microsoft 365, and every figure carries its source and the date it was read. After close, the same signals are watched every week against the baseline.

CREDITSWAN · HOW IT WORKS

06

Here is the mechanism. What comes in is what the firm already has: the deal room, which CreditSwan can read from the first day; the monthly reporting, forwarded the way it arrives; licensed and public outside evidence; and the firm's own past deals. Code rebuilds every figure from the schedules underneath, using the firm's definitions: the credit agreement for a lender, the plan for an owner. AI reads documents and drafts findings, and never calculates. Commercial claims are tested against the evidence and the firm's history. A named analyst signs every figure, ruling and alert before anything goes out. Results land where the team already works: the reply in the same Outlook thread, files and the portfolio view in SharePoint, the covenant or plan workbook in Excel, and Teams only when there is an exception. The example at the bottom is synthetic: a package reporting 3.4x leverage rebuilds to 3.9x against a 4.0x limit, because two add-backs fall outside the agreement's definitions.
